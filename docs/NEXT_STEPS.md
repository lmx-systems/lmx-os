| 24 | Package identity & scan-at-pickup verification (roadmap W10) | Raised by Sourabh, July 2026, reviewing the workflow doc: is there a unique barcode per package? There is not. `Stop.parcel_count`/`scanned_count` are two integers and `POST /driver/stops/{id}/scan` accepts `{scanned_count: int}` — a number, never a scanned value. No `Parcel` model, no barcode field, nothing. This also corrects **item A2/roadmap A2**, which reads like a hardware task ("wire in a camera SDK") but would deliver a scanner with nothing to scan. | Not started, **design decision open.** Two candidate models: LMX generates and applies its own labels (full control, needs a label printer per warehouse — unbudgeted capex, though co-location makes it practical) vs. scanning the distributor's existing pick-ticket barcode (no hardware, but only works where their Epicor prints one, which turns it into a hard sales-qualification requirement and compounds item 22's W8). A second decision underneath: does the ID describe an order or a package — `parcel_count` already implies one order can be several boxes. **Recommended sequencing:** resolve W8 first; if customer #1's Epicor already prints barcoded pick tickets, the scan-existing path is far cheaper and gets the win immediately. **Why it's worth doing:** `WRONG_PART` is currently caught at the shop door and the workflow session calls it "the most expensive recoverable error" — a scan-at-pickup check against the order catches it in the warehouse before the driver leaves. Also gives W1's returns/cores an identity, makes batched multi-order handoffs verifiable, and is the basis for real chain of custody (all four benchmarked competitors advertise it). |
