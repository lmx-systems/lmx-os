The counterintuitive part

Why holding an order makes it faster

Most dispatch works one way: an order comes in, it goes out. It feels fast, and it is the reason the industry runs at the efficiency it does.

LMX does the opposite on purpose. We hold an order for as long as we safely can, because a short wait lets us send it alongside others going the same direction. One driver, one run, several stops — instead of three separate trips.

Doing that safely requires knowing exactly how much time each order actually has, and re-checking that across the entire fleet every few seconds as traffic and new orders change the picture. That is what our system does, and it is the whole reason we can commit to delivery times when the operation we replace could not.

The delivery promise

Orders are committed by urgency tier and measured at the order level, not averaged into a monthly report. Performance is visible to you as it happens. When we miss, it is recorded with a reason and reviewed. Sustained failure against the thresholds in your contract triggers credits.

For the first time, you have a delivery operation whose payment depends on whether parts actually arrive on time.
